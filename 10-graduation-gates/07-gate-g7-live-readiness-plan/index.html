Capital-First Trading

Gate G7 live-readiness review and the one-page trading plan

Hook

You write a one-page plan. It covers your capital, your risk per trade, your daily limit and the amount you keep untouched. Then the form shows that your risk per trade is 6%. Hypothetical. The form fails it and says why. This gate checks the plan you wrote. It does not give permission to trade.

The idea

This gate asks you to fill in a written review form. The form has a fixed set of questions. Each one must be filled in.

This measures educational competence. It is not proof of profitability. Nothing here requires or recommends trading with real money.

The form asks for:

  • The capital you plan to use, in rupees.
  • The risk per trade, as a percentage of that capital.
  • Your daily, weekly and monthly loss limits, in rupees.
  • Your stop rules.
  • The most trades you will take in a day.
  • Your withdrawal policy.
  • The amount that stays untouched.

The risk per trade is checked against two levels from the risk tool in this book:

  • At or below 2%, the check passes.
  • Above 2%, the check fails, with a note.
  • Above 5%, the check fails, with the hard note from the risk tool.

The capital you enter is your own number. The book does not set a range for it and does not pick a number for you. It should be money you could lose without hardship. The capital chapters show how to work that out.

To pass, all of these must be true:

  1. Every field is filled in.
  2. The risk per trade is at or below 2%.
  3. The capital figure is filled in, and it is money you could lose without hardship.
  4. Gates G1 to G6 are all passed.

The one-page plan is made from the form. When you print it, you see the saved plan. The print is a view of the saved plan, not a second copy.

This gate does not set a waiting period. Even so, it is wise to put the plan away for a few days, then read it again before you rely on it.

You can edit the form and re-check it at any time. Each save has a date, and older versions stay in your history.

Passing all gates shows a message. It says you may consider a small real-money stage, and it adds caveats. It does not say you are ready to profit. Real money is your decision, made outside this book. You can stop at any time. There is no pressure to go live.

Passing this gate does not mean you should trade. It authorises nothing.

Worked example

Hypothetical. Capital Rs 50,000. Three different risk figures.

Risk per tradeRupees at risk on one tradeResult
1.5%50,000 x 1.5% = Rs 750Passes the risk check
3%50,000 x 3% = Rs 1,500Fails with a note
6%50,000 x 6% = Rs 3,000Fails with the hard note

For this capital, 2% is Rs 1,000 and 5% is Rs 2,500. The rupee levels only help you read the table. The form checks the percentage.

Try it

Guided exercise

Task. Hypothetical. Capital Rs 40,000. Risk per trade 2%. How many rupees is the risk per trade? Does it pass the risk check?

Hints:

  1. Multiply the capital by the percentage. Write 2% as 0.02.
  2. Compare the percentage with the 2% level.
  3. A figure at 2% is still at the level, not above it.

Answer: 40,000 x 0.02 = Rs 800. The figure is 2%, which is at the level, so the risk check passes.

Independent exercise

Task. Hypothetical. Capital Rs 40,000. Risk per trade 3%. How many rupees is the risk per trade? What does the form show?

Check your answer
  • Rupees at risk: 40,000 x 0.03 = Rs 1,200.
  • The figure is above 2% and below 5%. The risk check fails, and the form shows a note.
  • The note asks you to lower the risk per trade and check the figure again.

All figures are hypothetical.

Paper task

Mode: paper. Write it in your journal. If the journal is not ready, use paper.

  1. Write a one-page plan on paper with the seven fields from this chapter.
  2. Use made-up numbers only for this practice. Your real plan is a separate decision.
  3. Work out the risk per trade in rupees, using your capital and your percentage.
  4. Check the percentage against the 2% and 5% levels. Write the result beside it.
  5. Keep the page. Do not treat this practice plan as your real plan.

Limits and common mistakes

  • A written plan does not make you ready. It is one check among several.
  • The 2% and 5% levels are warning levels from the risk tool in this book. They are not market rules.
  • The gate does not cover tax. Tax rules are in Taxes on trading profits, and they have their own checks. The main rules, with their dates and sources, were read on 11 Nov 2026. Rates change with each Budget, so check incometax.gov.in each year and ask a qualified tax professional.
  • Passing gives a message with caveats. It does not authorise any trade.
  • Choosing your capital, risk and limits is a personal decision. The book does not choose a number for you.

Remember

  • The risk per trade must be at or below 2% to pass this check.
  • Every field of the form must be filled in.
  • All gates G1 to G6 must be passed first.
  • The printed plan shows the saved plan. It is not a separate copy.
  • Passing this gate does not mean you should trade. It authorises nothing.
