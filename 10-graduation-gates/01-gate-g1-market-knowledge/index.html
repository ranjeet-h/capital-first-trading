Capital-First Trading

Gate G1 market knowledge

Hook

Imagine you read a chapter on share prices and feel sure you understand it. Then a question asks what happens when more buyers than sellers want a share. You freeze. Feeling that you understand is not the same as being able to explain it. This gate checks whether you can explain the basics of the Indian market in your own words.

The idea

This gate covers the market basics from the first chapters of this book. It covers what a share is, how orders meet, how prices move, what an index is and what a corporate action does.

This measures educational competence. It is not proof of profitability. Nothing here requires or recommends trading with real money.

The gate has two parts. You must pass both.

  1. Quiz. You need at least 85% correct on the questions drawn for you.
  2. Own-words prompts. Each prompt has a short list of points. You tick each point you covered and write a short answer. A prompt is complete only when every point is ticked and your text box is not empty.

Some rules for the quiz:

  • Each set has at least 30 questions. This makes the 85% mark a fair measure.
  • Every topic in the earlier chapters appears at least once in each set.
  • The order of the answer choices changes each time.
  • You can retake straight away after you read the explanations. Each retake has new questions. There is no timer and no penalty.
  • An explanation is shown for every wrong answer. Read them before you retake.

Your attempts, your written answers and your result stay on your device. No result is sent anywhere.

Passing this gate shows that you can explain the market basics. It does not say you should trade. It does not unlock anything in this book.

Worked example

Hypothetical. A set of 30 questions. The pass mark is 85%.

85% of 30 is 25.5. You cannot get half a question right, so you need 26 correct.

Try it

Guided exercise

Task. A hypothetical set of 40 questions. How many correct answers are needed to reach 85%?

Hints:

  1. Multiply 40 by 0.85.
  2. If the answer is a decimal, round up to the next whole number.
  3. Check your answer. Divide it by 40. Is the result at least 85%?

Answer: 40 x 0.85 = 34. This is a whole number, so you need 34 correct answers. Check: 34 / 40 = 85.0%. Then 33 / 40 = 82.5%, which is not enough.

Independent exercise

Task. Write, in your own words and in no more than 40 words, what the order book shows. Then say what the difference between the bid and the ask is called.

Check your answer

A good answer covers these points:

  • The order book lists buy orders and sell orders at different prices.
  • The bid is the highest price a buyer is offering to pay right now.
  • The ask is the lowest price a seller is willing to accept right now.
  • The gap between the bid and the ask is called the spread.

Quiz

The quiz checks the pass mark, the rule for completing prompts and the basic market ideas.

Paper task

Mode: learning note. Write it in your journal. If the journal is not ready, use paper.

  1. On a blank page, write four short answers in your own words. Cover what a share is, what the order book shows, what a circuit limit is for and what an index is for.
  2. Read the matching chapter for each answer. Check your answer against it.
  3. Tick each point you covered. Rewrite any answer that is missing a point.
  4. Keep the finished page with your learning notes.

Limits and common mistakes

  • Passing shows that you can explain the basics. It does not show that you can read a chart or choose a trade.
  • The quiz draws from a pool of questions. A pass does not cover every fact in the book.
  • Market rules and timings can change. Check the exchange’s own pages before you rely on any detail.
  • Ticking a point you have not checked defeats the purpose. Read the chapter first, then tick.
  • A common mistake is to think that a familiar word means you understand it. Explain the word in your own sentence to test it.

Remember

  • You need at least 85% correct on the quiz, and every point ticked in each own-words prompt.
  • Each set has at least 30 questions.
  • Read the explanation for every wrong answer before you retake.
  • Passing this gate does not mean you should trade.
