## 2. My actual trading objective

This is one of the most important requirements and must influence the entire curriculum, strategy selection, risk management, practical exercises and learning progression.

I am NOT trying to become a high-income trader.

I am NOT interested in making huge profits, aggressive trading or maximizing returns.

My objective is modest, controlled and focused on protecting my capital.

My aspirational daily target:

- Desired daily profit: ₹2,000–₹3,000 maximum.
- Maximum acceptable daily loss: ₹2,000–₹3,000.
- I am comfortable earning nothing on days when there are no suitable opportunities.
- I do not want to take unnecessary risks to make a few hundred rupees.

These figures are planning boundaries, not guaranteed outcomes or mandatory daily targets.

### My trading philosophy

I would rather make smaller profits with controlled risk than chase large profits and expose my capital to substantial losses.

I do not want to:

- Risk ₹10,000 just to make ₹500.
- Take unnecessary trades because the market is open.
- Trade every day just to meet an income target.
- Increase position sizes after losses.
- Use leverage simply to increase potential returns.
- Average down blindly.
- Participate in strategies with small frequent profits but occasional catastrophic losses.
- Risk my household savings to generate trading income.

A no-trade day is perfectly acceptable.

If the market does not offer a suitable opportunity, I would rather earn nothing than lose money unnecessarily.

### Important: Do not confuse profit targets with risk limits

The ₹2,000–₹3,000 daily loss figure is an absolute planning ceiling, not a recommended amount to risk every day.

Teach me how to establish significantly smaller per-trade risks based on available trading capital.

Develop a risk management framework covering:

- Maximum risk per trade.
- Position sizing.
- Daily loss limits.
- Weekly loss limits.
- Monthly drawdown limits.
- Maximum number of trades.
- Consecutive losing trades.
- Rules for stopping trading.
- Rules for reducing exposure.
- Conditions under which trading should be suspended.

Once the daily loss limit is reached, trading must stop. No revenge trading or attempts to recover losses.

### Mathematical reality check

I specifically want a dedicated module that evaluates whether my ₹2,000–₹3,000 daily target is realistic.

Calculate and explain:

1. How much trading capital would be needed under different return assumptions?
2. What percentage daily return would be required?
3. What risk would be necessary to pursue those returns?
4. How much would brokerage, STT, GST, exchange charges and other costs reduce profits?
5. What happens after five, ten or fifteen consecutive losing days?
6. How much capital could be lost during unfavorable market conditions?
7. Why daily profit targets can encourage overtrading.
8. Why consistent daily returns cannot be guaranteed.

Show multiple realistic scenarios with different capital amounts.

Do not reverse-engineer an unrealistic strategy to make my target appear achievable.

If my desired returns are unreasonable relative to my available capital and acceptable risk, explain that clearly.

### Strategy selection criteria

Prioritize strategies with:

- Clearly defined downside.
- Reasonable capital requirements.
- Positive expectancy after transaction costs.
- Adequate liquidity.
- Repeatable rules.
- Manageable drawdowns.
- No dependence on excessive leverage.
- No requirement to monitor markets constantly.
- Robust performance across different market conditions.

Teach me why a strategy with a 90% win rate can still lose money.

Investigate the risks of scalping, intraday trading, option buying, option selling, averaging down and martingale-style position sizing.

Do not automatically recommend any strategy simply because it is popular.

### Capital protection

My existing savings and household financial security must remain completely separate from trading capital.

Develop a framework for deciding:

- How much capital is appropriate to allocate.
- How much money should remain untouched.
- What percentage of trading capital can be risked per trade.
- How to calculate position sizes.
- When to reduce trading capital.
- When to stop trading altogether.

Never assume I should deploy large capital just because I have savings.

# Trading Study Book: Curriculum and Build Plan

## Context
- Owner: senior software engineer, complete beginner in trading, Indian market (NSE/BSE), broker Zerodha Kite.
- Goal: learn to trade well over years, aiming for consistent profitability after costs. No promise of profit.
- Priority: capital preservation over returns. No scalping as a starter strategy. No F&O as an early activity.
- Learning first, then paper trading, then real money in small stages.
- Real-money stage amount: TBD by the owner.
- The book is educational. It must not place live orders. Real-money trading is the owner's decision, outside the book.

## Constraints for the coding agent
- Use official sources: SEBI, NSE, BSE, Zerodha support pages, Zerodha Varsity (reference only, do not copy text).
- Verify all Zerodha charges, plans and features against current official pages before writing them down. Record the verification date.
- Kite demo uses dummy data and is for interface practice only. Zerodha says it does not offer a paper-trading account.
- Kite Connect Personal API is free for orders, GTT and alerts, with no live or historical market data. The paid Connect plan adds market data.
- SEBI's retail algo framework became mandatory for brokers from 1 April 2026. Any content about automation must mention Algo-ID and broker compliance.
- Architecture: to be decided in planning. The owner prefers mdBook-style documentation. Do not use Rust or Svelte without an explicit, justified reason.
- Every calculation must be shown with rupee figures and a worked example.

## Curriculum

### Track 1: Foundations
1. How companies, exchanges, SEBI, NSE and BSE fit together
2. Demat and trading accounts; how a buy becomes a holding (T+1 settlement)
3. Order matching, bid-ask spread, liquidity, circuit limits
4. Reading candles, timeframes, support and resistance, trend

### Track 2: Money and math (highest priority)
5. Costs: brokerage, STT, GST, exchange and stamp charges, shown on a ₹5,000 trade and a ₹50,000 trade
6. Position sizing and stop-loss distance
7. Risk limits: per trade, daily, weekly, monthly
8. Expectancy, win rate, profit factor, max drawdown; why a 90% win rate can still lose money
9. The ₹2,000–₹3,000 daily target: capital needed, required daily return, effect of losing streaks

### Track 3: Kite and execution
10. Kite interface, watchlists, market depth, charts (demo account)
11. Order types (market, limit, SL, SL-M), product types (MIS vs CNC), GTT
12. Order book, trade book, holdings vs positions, cost preview
13. Common execution mistakes: wrong product type, stops that never trigger, fat-finger orders

### Track 4: Strategy and psychology
14. Trading styles compared: investing, swing, positional, intraday, F&O; SEBI F&O study as a case study
15. One fully specified swing strategy: setup, entry, stop, exit, sizing
16. Testing: backtesting, look-ahead bias, overfitting, out-of-sample tests
17. Psychology: loss aversion, revenge trading, FOMO, overconfidence, following a checklist

Each chapter: concept, visual explanation, worked example with rupee figures, interactive demo, guided exercise, independent exercise, quiz with explained answers, practical "paper" task.

## Hands-on labs
- Lab 1: Position-size calculator. Shares = (capital × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop). Show the example ₹50,000 account, 1% risk = ₹500.
- Lab 2: Cost calculator, using current published Zerodha charges with the verification date shown.
- Lab 3: Trade journal and statistics dashboard (spec below).
- Lab 4: Backtester with costs, walk-forward and out-of-sample splits. Show how look-ahead bias and survivorship bias change results.
- Lab 5: Forward-test script. Logs hypothetical trades each day using real prices. Places no orders.
