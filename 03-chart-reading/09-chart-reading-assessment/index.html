Capital-First Trading

Chart-reading assessment

Hook

A zone on a chart runs from Rs 249 to Rs 252. You plan to buy at Rs 252 and place a stop at Rs 247. You have Rs 50,000 and you risk 1 percent. Your planned loss looks small. Then the market opens at Rs 240, below your stop. The loss is bigger than the plan. This assessment checks whether you can work through numbers like these. It also checks whether you can read a chart you have not seen before.

The idea

This assessment has three parts.

  1. Calculation. You read open, high, low and close values. You work out the body, the shadow and the range. You turn a stop into a number of shares and a planned loss.
  2. Identification. You look at a chart you have not seen. You name the structure, any breakout and any gap.
  3. Scenario. You work through one trade with a stop and a gap.

Some terms used in the items:

  • Range is the high minus the low.
  • Body is the gap between the open and the close.
  • Shadow is the thin line above or below the body.
  • Gap is a jump in price between one trade and the next. The price opens away from the last close.
  • Risk per share is the entry price minus the stop price, for a buy.

Feedback follows every answer. Each wrong answer shows the rule that applies.

The pass mark is 80 percent for this chapter. With 6 questions, that means at least 5 correct. You can retake the assessment as often as you like. There is no timer.

The result screen says the same thing each time. Passing this chapter does not show that any chart method works. It shows only that you can read the chart items correctly.

See it

For the identification items, look at the chart first. Mark the highs and lows. Then name the structure. Check the gap, if any, before you answer. Do not look at the answer until you have written yours.

Worked example

Hypothetical. Zone item. Capital Rs 50,000. Risk 1 percent. Zone Rs 249 to Rs 252. Entry Rs 252. Stop Rs 247. The stop sits below the zone.

StepCalculationResult
1. Risk budget50,000 multiplied by 1%Rs 500
2. Risk per share252 minus 247Rs 5
3. Shares allowed by the risk budget500 divided by 5100
4. Shares allowed by cash50,000 divided by 252, rounded down198
5. Shares allowed by the 25 percent cap12,500 divided by 252, rounded down49
6. Sharesthe smallest of 100, 198 and 4949
7. Planned loss49 multiplied by Rs 5Rs 245

The 25 percent cap comes from Position sizing step by step. The concentration cap is the binding limit here.

Gap case. The market opens at Rs 240. You leave at the open price, which is Rs 240. The loss per share is 252 minus 240, which is Rs 12. Actual loss = 49 multiplied by Rs 12 = Rs 588.

Check the gap loss another way: 50 multiplied by 12 is 600. Take away 1 share at Rs 12 and you get Rs 588.

Lesson. The planned loss was Rs 245. The gap made the loss Rs 588. That is more than the Rs 500 risk budget. A stop is a trigger, not a guarantee of the price you get.

Try it

Guided exercise

Task. Capital Rs 50,000. Risk 1 percent. Zone Rs 500 to Rs 506. Entry Rs 506. Stop Rs 497. Work out the planned shares and the planned loss.

Hints:

  1. Identify the zone. Check that the stop sits outside it, and write the reason for the stop.
  2. Risk per share = entry minus stop.
  3. Take the smallest of the three share limits. Round each one down.

Answer:

  • Zone: Rs 500 to Rs 506. The stop at Rs 497 is below the zone.
  • Risk per share: 506 minus 497 = Rs 9.
  • Risk budget: Rs 500. Shares allowed: 500 divided by 9, rounded down, is 55.
  • Cash: 50,000 divided by 506, rounded down, is 98.
  • 25 percent cap: 12,500 divided by 506, rounded down, is 24.
  • Shares: the smallest is 24.
  • Planned loss: 24 multiplied by Rs 9 = Rs 216.
  • Check: 20 multiplied by 9 is 180. 4 multiplied by 9 is 36. 180 plus 36 is 216.

Independent exercise

Task. Work through this item with no hints. Capital Rs 50,000. Risk 1 percent. Zone Rs 600 to Rs 604. Entry Rs 604. Stop Rs 597. Find the planned shares, the planned loss and the loss if the market opens at Rs 590.

Check your answer
  • Risk per share: 604 minus 597 = Rs 7.
  • Risk budget: Rs 500. Shares allowed: 500 divided by 7, rounded down, is 71.
  • Cash: 50,000 divided by 604, rounded down, is 82.
  • 25 percent cap: 12,500 divided by 604, rounded down, is 20.
  • Shares: the smallest is 20.
  • Planned loss: 20 multiplied by Rs 7 = Rs 140.
  • Gap loss: risk per share at the open is 604 minus 590 = Rs 14. Loss = 20 multiplied by Rs 14 = Rs 280.
  • Check: 20 multiplied by 14 = 280. Rs 280 is below the Rs 500 risk budget.

All figures are hypothetical.

Limits and common mistakes

  • A passing score does not show that any chart method works. It shows only that you read the items correctly.
  • A stop is a trigger. A gap can open the price beyond your stop, so the loss can be bigger than planned.
  • Using the entry price instead of the stop price for risk per share gives the wrong loss. The stop sets the risk per share.
  • A stop placed inside a zone needs a stated reason. Without one, the stop is a guess.
  • Fast answers on sample items can feel like skill. Check your reasons, not only your score.

Remember

  • Risk per share = entry minus stop, for a buy.
  • Shares = the smallest of the risk limit, the cash limit and the cap. Round each one down.
  • A gap can make the actual loss bigger than the planned loss.
  • A passing score is not proof that any chart method works.
