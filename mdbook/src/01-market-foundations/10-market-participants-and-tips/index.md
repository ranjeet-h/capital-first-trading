# Market participants, where information comes from, and spotting misleading tips

## Hook

A headline says "foreign investors bought heavily today, so prices will rise." Hypothetical: on that day foreign investors bought Rs 4,000 crore and sold Rs 3,500 crore, which is a net buy of Rs 500 crore. The number is real, but it does not say which stocks were bought, why, or what happens next. Separately, a message in a group offers "guaranteed Rs 10,000 a month" on a Rs 50,000 account. That is a claim to be checked, not information to act on.

## The idea

Many kinds of people trade in the Indian market. Knowing who they are helps you read the data, not predict it.

- **Retail investors:** individuals trading their own money.
- **Institutions:** funds, insurers and banks. They have research teams and different cost structures. [VERIFY: participant category definitions, SEBI or NSE]
- **FII / FPI:** foreign investors. **DII:** domestic institutional investors. [VERIFY: definitions]
- **Proprietary traders and market makers:** firms trading their own capital, or quoting prices on both sides of the market. [VERIFY: definitions]

**Net flow.** For a group of participants, net flow = buy value minus sell value. A positive figure is net buying, a negative figure is net selling. FII and DII data is published daily by the exchange. [VERIFY: FII/DII data source and publication timing, NSE FII/DII page]

**What a net flow tells you.** The total money moved by one group on one day.

**What a net flow does not tell you.** Which stocks were bought, why, what the buyers expected, or what the price will do next.

**The information ladder.** Sources sit on three levels:

- **Primary:** the company's own filings, exchange announcements, and regulator orders. These are the original documents.
- **Secondary:** news reports, analysis and research that repeat or interpret primary information.
- **Unverified:** tips, forwarded messages and social media posts with no checkable source.

Start from primary sources. Use secondary sources to add context. Treat unverified claims as claims to check, not facts.

**Signs of a misleading tip:**
- guaranteed or promised returns,
- urgency, such as "join now or lose the chance",
- pressure to join a group or pay for a signal,
- no registration details,
- claims of insider information.

**Advisers and research analysts** must be registered with SEBI. [VERIFY: registration categories and public lookup, SEBI intermediaries page] Being in a messaging group proves nothing about registration.

**The book does not recommend stocks, and no source in this chapter is an endorsement.**

## Worked example

Hypothetical day, dummy numbers.

**Net flow.**

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FII buy value | given | Rs 4,000 crore |
| FII sell value | given | Rs 3,500 crore |
| Net flow | 4,000 - 3,500 | Rs 500 crore net buying |

The figure shows the total flow. It does not show which stocks were bought or why.

**Tip claim.** "Guaranteed Rs 10,000 a month from my signals" on a Rs 50,000 account.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly amount | given | Rs 10,000 |
| Account size | given | Rs 50,000 |
| Monthly return implied | 10,000 / 50,000 x 100 | 20% a month |

The book treats a claim of 20% a month, with a guarantee, as a red flag. It is not information to act on.

### Guided exercise

**Task.** Classify these five hypothetical messages as primary, secondary or unverified, with a reason for each.

1. A company's filing on the exchange website, announcing a board meeting date.
2. A business news article that summarises that filing.
3. A forwarded message: "Guaranteed 3x in a month, join now, only 10 seats left."
4. A SEBI order published on SEBI's website.
5. A social media post with a screenshot of a broker statement and no source given.

Hints:
1. Who published it: the company, the exchange, the regulator, or someone repeating it?
2. Is it an original document, an interpretation, or an unsourced claim?
3. Can you check it against an official page?

**Answer:**
1. Primary. It is the company's own filing on the exchange.
2. Secondary. It repeats and summarises a primary source.
3. Unverified. No source, and it carries guaranteed returns and urgency.
4. Primary. It is a regulator's own order.
5. Unverified. The screenshot has no checkable source, so it is a claim to check.

## Paper task

**Mode: learning note.** No action on tips.

1. Create a journal entry titled "Information sources I used".
2. For each source you read this week, log the date, the source name, its type (primary, secondary or unverified) and its verification status.
3. Mark any unverified claim as "not checked".
4. Take no action on any tip, group or signal service.
5. Log it in your journal, or on paper until the journal app is ready.

## What this does NOT tell you

- A net flow figure shows total money moved. It does not show which stocks, why, or what happens next.
- A registration check is not an endorsement of the adviser's views.
- The book does not recommend stocks, groups, or signal services.
- A source being "official-looking" is not verification. Check the primary document.
- Scams often use guaranteed returns and pressure to act quickly. Treat both as warning signs, not as information.

## Remember

- Net flow = buy value minus sell value. It is an aggregate, not a stock pick.
- Start from primary sources. Use secondary sources for context. Treat unverified claims as claims.
- Red flags: guaranteed returns, urgency, payment for signals, group pressure, insider claims, no registration.
- Being in a group does not mean an adviser is registered. Check the SEBI list.
- Doing nothing is an allowed and often correct result.
