# Market participants and misleading tips

## Hook

A headline says, "Foreign investors bought heavily today, so prices will rise." This is a hypothetical example. On that day, foreign investors bought Rs 4,000 crore of shares. They sold Rs 3,500 crore. So the net buying was Rs 500 crore. The number is real. But it does not say which stocks were bought, or why, or what happens next. A message in a group also offers "assured Rs 10,000 a month" on a Rs 50,000 account. That is a claim to check. It is not information to act on.

## The idea

Many kinds of people trade in the Indian market. Knowing who they are helps you read the data. It does not help you predict prices.

- **Retail investors:** individuals trading their own money.
- **Institutions:** funds, insurers and banks. They have research teams and different cost structures.
- **FII / FPI:** foreign investors. **DII:** domestic institutional investors. These are plain descriptions, not legal definitions. The legal terms are in SEBI's rules.
- **Proprietary traders and market makers:** firms that trade their own money. Some firms also post buy and sell prices at the same time.

**Net flow.** For a group of participants, net flow = buy value minus sell value. A positive figure is net buying. A negative figure is net selling. NSE publishes FII and DII trading figures on its website, and the page offers downloads. The figures are provisional. NSE builds them from the trading codes that its members enter, and final foreign investor data comes later from the depository, NSDL. NSE's page gives the latest day's data, but this chapter does not state the exact hour it appears. (As of 11 Nov 2026, NSE FII/DII trading activity page. Check the NSE site.)

**What a net flow tells you.** The total money one group moved on one day.

**What a net flow does not tell you.** Which stocks were bought. Why they were bought. What the buyers expected. What the price will do next.

**The information ladder.** Sources sit on three levels:

- **Primary:** the company's own filings, exchange announcements, and regulator orders. These are the original documents.
- **Secondary:** news reports, analysis and research. They repeat or explain primary information.
- **Unverified:** tips, forwarded messages and social media posts. They have no source you can check.

Start from primary sources. Use secondary sources for context. Treat unverified claims as claims to check, not as facts.

**Signs of a misleading tip:**
- assured or promised returns,
- urgency, such as "join now or lose the chance",
- pressure to join a group or pay for a signal,
- no registration details,
- claims of insider information.

**Advisers and research analysts** must be registered with SEBI. SEBI keeps public lists of registered Research Analysts and Investment Advisers on its website. SEBI's investor site also has a tool to check the registration status of any market intermediary, and a tool called SEBI Check to confirm that a payment ID or account belongs to a registered intermediary. SEBI has also told regulated firms not to work with people who give securities advice without registration, or who promise returns without permission. People who only teach investing are left out of that rule. (As of 11 Nov 2026, SEBI investor support page and SEBI circular of 29 Jan 2025, updated 8 May 2026. Rules change, so check the SEBI site.) Being in a messaging group proves nothing about registration.

**The book does not recommend stocks. No source in this chapter is an endorsement.**

|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Company filing, exchange announcement, SEBI order | Checking the facts | Check the date and the issuer |
| Secondary | News article, analysis report | Context and cross-checking | Check the author's method and any conflicts |
| Unverified | Tip, forwarded message, social media post | Nothing, until checked against a primary source | Assured returns, urgency, or payment for signals |

## Worked example

This is a hypothetical day with made-up numbers.

**Net flow.**

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FII buy value | given | Rs 4,000 crore |
| FII sell value | given | Rs 3,500 crore |
| Net flow | 4,000 - 3,500 | Rs 500 crore net buying |

The figure shows the total flow. It does not show which stocks were bought, or why.

**Tip claim.** "Assured Rs 10,000 a month from my signals" on a Rs 50,000 account.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly amount | given | Rs 10,000 |
| Account size | given | Rs 50,000 |
| Monthly return implied | 10,000 / 50,000 x 100 | 20% a month |

The book treats a promised 20% a month, with a guarantee, as a red flag. It is not information to act on.

### Guided exercise

**Task.** Sort these five made-up messages into primary, secondary or unverified. Give a reason for each.

1. A company's filing on the exchange website, announcing a board meeting date.
2. A business news article that sums up that filing.
3. A forwarded message: "Assured 3x in a month, join now, only 10 seats left."
4. A SEBI order published on SEBI's website.
5. A social media post with a screenshot of a broker statement and no source given.

Hints:
1. Who published it: the company, the exchange, the regulator, or someone repeating it?
2. Is it an original document, an explanation, or a claim with no source?
3. Can you check it against an official page?

**Answer:**
1. Primary. It is the company's own filing on the exchange.
2. Secondary. It repeats and sums up a primary source.
3. Unverified. No source, and it carries assured returns and urgency.
4. Primary. It is a regulator's own order.
5. Unverified. The screenshot has no source you can check, so it is a claim to check.

### Independent exercise

**Task.** Write a checklist of six steps to check a tip before you act on it. Include "do nothing" as an allowed result.

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

A sample checklist:

1. Find the original source of the claim.
2. Find the primary document (filing, exchange announcement or regulator order) that supports it.
3. Check whether the person or firm is SEBI-registered, using the official lookup on the SEBI website.
4. Check for red flags: assured returns, urgency, payment for signals, group pressure.
5. Write down what would prove the claim wrong, and check for it.
6. Decide. "Do nothing" is an allowed result. Do not act on a tip alone.

## Limits and common mistakes

- A net flow figure shows total money moved. It does not show which stocks, why, or what happens next.
- A registration check is not an endorsement of the adviser's views.
- The book does not recommend stocks, groups, or signal services.
- A source that looks official is not verified. Check the primary document.
- Scams often use assured returns and pressure to act fast. Treat both as warning signs, not as information.

## Remember

- Net flow = buy value minus sell value. It is a total for a group, not a stock pick.
- Start from primary sources. Use secondary sources for context. Treat unverified claims as claims.
- Red flags: assured returns, urgency, payment for signals, group pressure, insider claims, no registration.
- Being in a group does not mean an adviser is registered. Check the SEBI list.
- Doing nothing is an allowed result. Often it is the right one.
