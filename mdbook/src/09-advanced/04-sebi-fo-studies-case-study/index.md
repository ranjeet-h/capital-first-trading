# SEBI's F&O studies as a case study

## Hook

A headline says, "Most F&O traders lose money." You read it and think, "So I will lose too." One headline percentage does not tell you what will happen to you. This chapter shows what a study of traders can say, and what it cannot say.

## The idea

F&O means futures and options. A **study** of traders counts what happened to a group of people over a period. It describes the past. It does not forecast what will happen to one person.

Before you quote any study, ask these five method questions.

1. Which people were in the sample, and how were they chosen?
2. Which period did the study cover?
3. Were costs included in the gains and losses?
4. How were gains and losses measured?
5. Did people leave the study, and were the same people followed over time?

**Expected value** is the average result you would get over many similar tries. A percentage of losers does not give that average by itself. You also need the size of the wins and the size of the losses, together.

**Survivorship** is a bias that counts only people who stayed in the sample. People who left are missing from the totals. A study can then look better than the full group. See *Survivorship bias*.

**Quoting figures.** Quote a figure only from its source, with the study name, the date and the period. Never quote a figure from memory. [VERIFY: exact study title, publication date, period and headline figures]

## See it

Use this checklist before you read any headline. Read the method section of the report first, then the headline.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who was in the sample? | A small or chosen group may not represent others. |
| Which period? | A short or unusual period can mislead. |
| Are costs included? | Costs change the net result. |
| How are gains and losses measured? | Different measures give different totals. |
| Did people leave, and were the same people tracked? | People who left can be missing, which changes the picture. |

## Worked example

**Hypothetical group.** 1,000 traders. 600 had net losses. 400 had net gains.

| Group | Number | Total result | Average per trader |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winners | 400 | Rs 4,00,000 gain | Rs 1,000 gain |
| Losers | 600 | Rs 12,00,000 loss | Rs 2,000 loss |
| Whole group | 1,000 | Rs 8,00,000 net loss | Rs 800 net loss |

Check the whole group: Rs 4,00,000 - Rs 12,00,000 = minus Rs 8,00,000. Divide by 1,000 traders to get minus Rs 800 each.

A percentage alone seems to show a poor result. A different hypothetical group with 400 losers and larger average wins could show a positive net result. The lesson is to read the totals and the method, not one percentage.

## Try it

### Guided exercise

**Task.** Using the worked example, compute the average gain per winner and the average loss per loser. State which average is larger.

Hints:

1. The average gain per winner is Rs 4,00,000 divided by 400.
2. The average loss per loser is Rs 12,00,000 divided by 600.
3. Compare the two averages.

**Answer:** Average gain per winner = Rs 4,00,000 / 400 = **Rs 1,000**. Average loss per loser = Rs 12,00,000 / 600 = **Rs 2,000**. The average loss is larger. That is why the whole group nets a loss, even though 400 traders gained.

## Limits and common mistakes

- This chapter quotes no SEBI figure. Check each figure at its source. [VERIFY: SEBI research and studies page, exact title, date, period, sample and headline figures]
- A study describes a group over a period. It does not forecast any one trader.
- Even a sound study does not show that your own trades will match the group.
- Headlines often drop the period, the sample or the costs. Check the method first.
- Do not quote a figure from memory, even when you remember it well.

## Remember

- A study describes a group over a period. It does not forecast you.
- A percentage of losers does not give an expected value by itself.
- Check the sample, period, costs and measures before you quote a study.
- Quote figures from the source, with the date and period.
- Survivorship can make a study look better than the full group.
