# When to stop trading altogether

## Hook

Your monthly loss limit is Rs 3,000 on a Rs 50,000 account. In the third week you reach it. You feel that one more trade will fix things and bring the month back on track. This is the moment a written rule matters most. This chapter explains the stopping levels in a plan. It also shows how to restart without rushing, and why stopping is part of the method.

## The idea

A plan uses three levels of stopping:

1. **Stop for the day.** Trading ends for the day when the daily limit is reached.
2. **Stop for the week.** Trading ends for the week when the weekly limit is reached.
3. **Suspend until review.** Trading stops until you have reviewed the cause, when a serious trigger is reached.

**Suspension triggers** are the conditions that move you to a stop. Each trigger must be a yes or no test. They can include:

- a limit breach,
- a set number of losses in a row, with the number written in your plan,
- a drop from your peak account value,
- a rule that was broken,
- a feeling you cannot manage.

**Reducing exposure** means cutting the risk per trade, or the capital at risk, by a written step. The step is set in the plan. It is not chosen by feel. After a drop from peak, a written cut in size often comes before a full stop. The smaller size keeps you active while the loss is still small.

**Restart** needs three things:

- A written review of the cause.
- A waiting period that you write in advance. You choose its length.
- A plan change if the cause was a broken rule.

Stopping is part of the method. It is not a failure.

## Worked example

**Hypothetical.** Capital Rs 50,000. The limits below are illustrative. You set your own real figures.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Monthly loss limit | 6% of Rs 50,000 | Rs 3,000 |
| 2. Trigger point | cumulative monthly loss reaches Rs 3,000 | Trading suspended |
| 3. Drop from peak | 50,000 - 45,000 | Rs 5,000 (10% of Rs 50,000) |
| 4. Risk before the cut | 1% of Rs 50,000 | Rs 500 |
| 5. Risk after the cut | 0.5% of Rs 45,000 | Rs 225 |

After a 10% drop from peak, the written step halves the risk percentage, from 1% to 0.5%. In rupees, the risk per trade falls from Rs 500 to Rs 225. The cut is measured on the current capital of Rs 45,000, not on the old peak.

## Try it

### Guided exercise

**Task.** Work out the monthly loss limit in rupees for a 5% limit on Rs 40,000.

Hints:

1. 5% of 40,000.
2. Then work out the risk per trade at 1% of the capital left after a full loss of that limit.

**Answer:**

- Monthly loss limit: 5% x Rs 40,000 = **Rs 2,000**.
- If you lose the full Rs 2,000, capital falls to 40,000 - 2,000 = Rs 38,000.
- Risk per trade at 1% of Rs 38,000 = **Rs 380**. Check: 0.01 x 38,000 = 380.

## Limits and common mistakes

- The 6% and 5% figures in this chapter are illustrations, not defaults. Set your own limits in writing before you trade.
- A limit does not stop a loss inside one trade. If the price gaps past your stop, one loss can be bigger than the planned 1R. See *Slippage*.
- Limits apply to your trading money only. Money you need for daily life, such as household savings, stays outside any trading loss limit.
- Stopping does not mean giving up on trading. It is a pause that protects your capital.
- If trading causes you real distress, talk to a trusted person or a professional. Do not wait for a limit to be reached before you do this.

## Remember

- Three levels: stop for the day, stop for the week, suspend until review.
- Each suspension trigger is a yes or no test.
- Reduce exposure by a written step, not by feel.
- A restart needs a written review, a waiting period you wrote in advance, and a plan change if a rule broke.
- Stopping is part of the method.
